## Pilot for Mac {{VERSION}}

**He moves around on his own now; pick Calm in his menu to make him rest.**

Pilot is the MTRX Horizyn AI assistant: a friendly robot that sits on top of everything on your Mac. Click it to chat, get help with writing and code, or have it look at your screen when you ask. It runs on your own AI key (OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, or any compatible API), so your AI provider bills you directly and MTRX never sees your key.

One file for every Mac: Apple Silicon and Intel, macOS 12 or newer.

### What's new in 1.2.0

- **Always moving is the new setting.** The robot walks and runs along the bottom of your screen, flies across to another spot and lands like a superhero, and climbs up the screen edges and drops or flies back down. He goes somewhere new every time, in both directions, with only short pauses, sometimes a look at your mouse or a wave. About every couple of minutes he does a trick or a sport: a backflip, a cheer, a knee slide, football, soccer, tennis or an energy ball.
- **Energy now has three choices: Calm, Relaxed and Always moving.** Calm: he rests. Relaxed: a move now and then, like Pilot 1.1. Always moving: he wanders on his own. Pick one from the robot head in the menu bar, by right-clicking the robot, or in settings. If you had turned Calm Mode on, you keep Calm. If your Mac is set to reduce motion, he rests unless you pick another choice.
- **He still keeps out of your way.** Clicks go straight through to your apps except on the robot himself, he never covers the menu bar, and he doesn't stop where your mouse is. He stays put next to the chat while it's open, and keeps still while a video or game is full screen, while you drag him, while he's hidden, and while you're away from your Mac or it's locked. He waves when you come back.
- **Light on your Mac.** He moves with the same smooth, low-power animations as before, with fewer glow effects while he travels.

### Install in 4 steps

1. Open the downloaded **.dmg** file.
2. Drag **Pilot** onto **Applications**.
3. Open **Pilot** from Applications. If your Mac says it can't check Pilot, press **Done**.
4. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down, press **Open Anyway** next to Pilot, then **Open**.

Step 4 is only needed once, because Pilot isn't signed with an Apple developer account yet. Already have an older Pilot? Quit it from the menu bar, then drag the new Pilot onto Applications and choose Replace.
