Orbyt: work with MTRX Horizyn

Orbyt is where you can ask to partner with us, pitch an idea, get in touch as an investor, or ask about buying the company or one of its apps. Until it opened, this site kept nothing that visitors typed.{% if not (early or creators) %} It is still the only place that keeps anything you give it, and only once you press Send.{% endif %}

We keep what you typed into the form and nothing more: your name, your email address and your message; your organisation, if you gave one; for a partnership, which apps and the kind of partnership; for an idea, its name, its one-line pitch and who it's for; for buying, whether it's the whole company or which apps. We also keep the time it arrived and which of the four paths you chose. We don't keep your network address with it, and no cookie is set.

We use it only to read your message and reply to you. It is never shared, sold or used for marketing, and only MTRX Horizyn's owner can read it, in the site's private console. When email alerts are set up, the owner gets a short note that a message arrived, saying only which path and the organisation or idea name (and, for buying, which apps or the company); your email address and message stay on the site. Each message is deleted {{ vortex_days }} days after it arrives, or sooner once we've dealt with it. To have yours deleted sooner, ask us when we reply.

To stop the form being misused, we count how many messages each visitor sends: the address the message came from (for an IPv6 address, the block it belongs to) and the time are kept for 24 hours, then deleted. If you're under 13, please ask a parent or guardian to send it for you.

What the server logs

For each request the server writes one line: the time, the network address the request came from, the page asked for and whether it worked. Behind our hosting provider, that address is usually the provider's own rather than yours. Our hosting provider keeps its own records of the requests it passes on, which can include your IP address, for as long as its own policy says. We use these lines only to keep the site working and safe, and never to work out who anyone is.

Failed sign-ins

Parts of the site are private. If someone tries a code or a sign-in there and it fails, the address it came from (for an IPv6 address, the block it belongs to) and the time are kept for 24 hours, so nobody can try codes without limit. Then they are deleted.

Cookies

Visitors get none. The site sets cookies only on the owner's own approved devices, to keep the private console locked. They are needed for that and for nothing else, are never readable by the page's own scripts, never go to any other site, and are not used to track anyone:

  • mh_d remembers that a device is approved, for up to {{ cookies.device_days }} days.
  • mh_k lasts {{ cookies.door_minutes }} minutes, for one step of signing in.
  • mh_s keeps the owner signed in, for up to {{ cookies.session_hours }} hours.

What stays in your browser

If you turn on calm mode in Settings, your browser remembers that choice on your device. It is never sent to us. The search box searches this page inside your browser; what you type is not sent anywhere.

Genesis projects stay in your browser, except the text you choose to send with "Ask Genesis", which goes to our AI provider to write the answer; we keep only counts. Your projects are saved in your browser's storage for this site, on your device only, until you delete them (each one, or Delete all) or clear this site's data. Download saves them as a file on your device, and Import reads that file back in your browser; neither sends anything to us. We can't see, recover or delete your projects for you.

Your answers to Genesis's daily spark, and your streak, are kept the same way: in your browser only, never sent to us, until you press Clear my sparks or clear this site's data. An idea card is drawn in your browser too; it goes only where you save or share it.

"Pitch this in Orbyt" tells the Orbyt page which project to fill in, through your browser's storage for that tab only. The project's words are never put in a web address, and nothing is sent until you press Send.

Ask Genesis

If you've added your own AI key in Pilot, Ask Genesis uses it instead: the same few fields go from your browser straight to your AI company, nothing goes to our server, and there's no daily limit. Otherwise:

Ask Genesis is optional AI help with your idea. Only when you press one of its buttons, your project's name, one-line pitch, who it's for and stage, and any question you typed, go through our server to our AI provider, Anthropic, which writes the answer. Your notes and checklist are never sent. We don't store or log that text, and the answer comes straight back to your browser. Our AI provider handles it under its own terms and privacy policy for businesses that use its service. Please don't type personal details into Genesis.

To share the free help out fairly, we count how many questions each visitor asks: the address the question came from (for an IPv6 address, the block it belongs to) and the time are kept for 24 hours, then deleted. We also keep how much each answer cost, as a number with no words in it. Nothing else about you is kept, and no cookie is set.

Pilot

Pilot is an optional assistant on this site's pages. It does nothing until you add your own AI key from an AI company you choose (such as Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, or another service that works the same way, including one on your own computer). AI keys are for grown-ups: the AI companies require you to be 18 or over.

Your key is kept only in your browser: for this tab, or on this device if you tick "Remember". Remove my keys deletes it. When you send Pilot a message, your browser sends it, with the conversation so far and a short guide to this site, straight to that AI company, which answers under its own terms and privacy policy and bills your key. Nothing goes through our server and we never see your key, your messages or the answers. "Analyze & explain" and "Summarise" also send an outline of the words on the screen; Pilot never reads what you type into boxes or hidden parts of the page{% if pro %}, or your Genesis Pro key{% endif %}, and it blanks out email addresses and phone numbers. On the Genesis page, Pilot can read your projects to answer you, and changes them only after you press Allow. With an image or video key, pictures and videos are made by that company and kept only on the page until you download them.

The chat is kept in your browser for this tab, so it follows you from page to page. Memory ("remember that I like short answers") is off unless you turn it on, and then stays only in this browser. Voice uses your browser's own speech recognition, if it has one: nothing goes to us, but some browsers send the sound to their maker to turn it into words.

Orbyt Pitch Review

Pitch Review is an optional paid extra for a pitch sent in Orbyt, bought by a grown-up through Stripe, our payment provider. Stripe takes the payment and the email address on its own checkout page, under its own privacy policy. Card details never reach us.

When you send a pitch, your browser is given a one-time code for that pitch, so the page can offer a review; we keep only a fingerprint of the code, for 2 days. When Stripe tells us a review has been paid for, we keep: which pitch it's for, the idea's name, the email address used at checkout (to send the review to), the amount, and when it was paid, when it's due, and when it was sent or refunded. We email that address a confirmation, and the owner a short note with only the idea's name and the due date.

The pitch itself is kept like any Orbyt message (above), except that a pitch with a review still to write isn't deleted before the review is done. The email address and the idea's name are deleted {{ review_forget_days }} days after the review is sent or refunded, and the rest of the order (the dates and the amount, for our accounts) after {{ review_order_days }} days.

To stop misuse, starting a checkout is limited too: the address a request came from (for an IPv6 address, the block it belongs to) and the time are kept for 24 hours, then deleted.

Genesis Pro

Genesis Pro is an optional paid upgrade to Genesis, bought by a grown-up through Stripe, our payment provider. Stripe takes the payment and the email address on its own checkout page, under its own privacy policy. Card details never reach us.

From Stripe we keep: its codes for the customer and the subscription; whether Pro is paid up; monthly or yearly; the price; when it renews or ends; and each payment and refund. Instead of the email address, we keep a fingerprint of it (a one-way code that can't be turned back into the address), so that "Email a new key" can send a key to the same address. When we email a key, the address is used to send it and is not kept.

Your Pro key is kept in your browser's storage for this site and sent with Pro requests, in the request rather than as a cookie. We keep only a fingerprint of each key.

Pro's AI help works like Ask Genesis above, except that questions are counted by member instead of by address: which member asked, when, and what the answer cost, kept for 32 days. The words are never kept.

Cloud backup. While a browser has Pro, it sends your Genesis projects (names, pitches, checklists and notes) to our server to keep a backup: the last 3 copies, for you alone to restore. Nobody else can see them, and they are never used for anything else. Delete them any time with Delete backups; otherwise they are deleted {{ pro_backup_days }} days after Pro ends.

To stop misuse, Pro's buttons are limited too: the address a request came from (for an IPv6 address, the block it belongs to) and the time are kept for 24 hours, then deleted.
